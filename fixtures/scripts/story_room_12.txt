The guest in room 12

For six years, I worked the night desk at the Sundowner, a 40-room motel on Route 66 in Amarillo, Texas. Nights were quiet. I did crosswords, knitted scarves nobody asked for, and texted my wife to make sure she was still awake.

Every year on March 3rd, the same man checked in. Mr. Alvarez was maybe seventy, with a pressed shirt and one small suitcase. He always asked for room 12 and always paid $38 in cash, which was our price in the 1990s, and my manager Deb always let him.

One year, I finally asked him why room 12. He smiled and said, "Because in 1974, it was the only room left, and she said yes anyway." He and his wife had spent their honeymoon there. She had died in 2016, and March 3rd was her birthday.

After that, I kept room 12 ready for him. Fresh flowers from the grocery store, the good towels, the heater turned on an hour early. He never mentioned it, but he always left a thank-you note on the pillow.

This March, he didn't come. By midnight, I texted Deb: "Mr. Alvarez isn't here. Should I worry?" She wrote back: "Keep the room. He's never missed one."

At 1:40 in the morning, a woman walked in holding a small suitcase I recognized. She introduced herself as Sofia, his daughter. Her father had passed away in January, and he had left her a list of things to do. Number four was "Stay in room 12 on March 3rd and thank the night clerk."

She handed me an envelope. Inside was a photo of her parents standing outside room 12 in 1974, the sign behind them exactly the same as it is today, and a note in his handwriting: "Thank you for keeping the heater on. She always said this place was warm."

Sofia stayed the night. In the morning, she paid for room 12 for next March 3rd, and for the one after that. She says she plans to come every year.

I don't work nights anymore. Last fall, I bought the Sundowner from Deb. Room 12 is never for rent on March 3rd.
