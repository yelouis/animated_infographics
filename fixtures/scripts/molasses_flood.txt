The Great Molasses Flood

On January 15, 1919, in Boston's North End, a giant steel tank holding 2.3 million gallons of molasses burst open. A wave of syrup up to 25 feet high rushed through the streets at 35 miles per hour. It knocked buildings off their foundations and trapped horses, workers, and children.

Twenty-one people died and about 150 were injured. Rescuers waded through waist-deep molasses for days, and the harbor stayed brown until summer.

Investigators found that the tank had been built in a hurry, never properly tested, and had leaked from the start. The company had painted it brown to hide the leaks. After the court case, Massachusetts began requiring engineers to sign off on construction plans.
