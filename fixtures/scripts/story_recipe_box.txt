My grandmother left me her recipe box, and the most important card was missing

As the only granddaughter, I got Grandma Rose's recipe box when she died in 2013. My brother Danny got her house in Duluth, and honestly, I think I got the better deal. The box held 312 handwritten cards, forty years of cooking at the little diner she ran on Superior Street.

Every card was there except one. Rose's blueberry pie was the reason people lined up outside the diner on Sundays, and where its card should have been, there was a slip of paper with two words on it: "Ask Walt."

Nobody in the family knew a Walt. Danny thought it was a joke. My mom thought it was a supplier. I kept that slip on my fridge for eleven years.

Last spring, Danny finally sold the house. While clearing the attic, he found a shoebox of letters and texted me a photo: "Who is Walter Lindqvist and why did he write to Grandma 60 times?" The return address was in Thunder Bay, Ontario, about 190 miles up the shore.

I called the number I found online, expecting nothing. A man answered, and when I said Rose's name, he was quiet for a long time. Then he said, "I've been waiting for someone to call about the pie."

I drove up that weekend. Walt was 91, sharp as a tack, and living alone with a very old cat. He told me that he and Rose had been engaged in 1961, until his family moved north and the engagement fell apart. Before he left, his mother gave Rose her blueberry pie recipe as a goodbye gift, on one condition: it stayed in the family. Rose kept that promise for over fifty years. She never wrote the recipe down. She baked it from memory and mailed Walt a photo of every Sunday pie.

Then Walt opened a kitchen drawer and handed me the missing card. Rose had mailed it to him the week before she died, with a note: "It was always yours. Give it to whoever comes asking."

I asked if he minded me using it. He laughed and said I was the closest thing to family that recipe had left.

Last month, I reopened the diner on Superior Street. The first slice of blueberry pie went to Walt, who made Danny drive him down for the occasion. He says it tastes exactly right.
